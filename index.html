How to Use

Day-to-day workflow inside the Leads sheet

Search

NCCB CRM > Search Leads opens a sidebar. Type a name, phone, email, city, or Lead ID and press Enter. Click any result to jump straight to that lead's row. The filter arrows already on row 1 of the Leads tab work the same way for a quick on-sheet sort or filter.

Make a Note

Click any cell in a lead's row, then NCCB CRM > Add Note to Selected Lead. Type the note and click OK. It's written straight to the Notes Log tab with the timestamp and your email attached automatically — no need to type either yourself.

Automated Save (why notes lock)

The moment a note is saved, it starts moving toward being locked — permanent and uneditable, even by the rep who wrote it. That's on purpose. The point of the notes log is to be a record everyone can trust: the next rep who picks up a lead, or Lawrence reviewing a file, needs to see exactly what was said and disclosed, in the order it happened, with nothing smoothed over or corrected after the fact. That matters both for handoffs between reps and for showing, if it's ever asked, what a consumer was actually told and when. Write notes the way you'd want to read them if someone else picked up this lead tomorrow.

Push an Appointment

Click a lead's row, then NCCB CRM > Push Appointment to Calendar. Enter the date, time, and duration. It lands on your own Google Calendar and sends an invite to the lead and the assigned rep automatically.

Definitions

Terms used across the Leads sheet, scripts, and compliance references
Lead ID
The unique identifier (e.g. L00001) assigned to each record on the Leads tab. Used to match notes and appointments back to the right person.
McGraw Leads
The lead vendor/source for this list.
90-180 Day Aged Lead
A lead that came in 90 to 180 days ago and has not yet converted — typically needs re-engagement rather than a first-touch pitch.
TrustedForm
A third-party certificate attached to a lead at the moment it was submitted online, showing that the consumer's consent to be contacted was captured. It is the paper trail behind the phone call, not a guarantee of consent for every future contact channel.
Assigned Rep
The team member currently responsible for working a lead. Drives the calendar invite when an appointment is pushed.
Priority
Auto-calculated tier (High / Medium / Standard) based on the lead's Total Debt.
Status
Where a lead sits in the pipeline — New, Attempted Contact, Left Voicemail, Contacted, Callback Scheduled, Qualified, Documents Sent, Enrolled, Do Not Call, or Dead Lead.
Notes Log
The permanent, append-only record of every note added to every lead. See "Automated Save" under How to Use.
DMP — Debt Management Plan
A structured repayment plan, arranged through a licensed provider, that consolidates a consumer's unsecured debts into one monthly payment, often at a reduced interest rate negotiated with creditors.
OCCC — Office of Consumer Credit Commissioner
The Texas state agency that licenses and regulates debt management service providers under Texas Finance Code Chapter 394.
CRO — Credit Repair Organization
A business that offers, for a fee, to improve a consumer's credit record or credit history. Regulated federally under the Credit Repair Organizations Act (CROA).
Credit/BK Counseling
Credit counseling and pre-bankruptcy financial counseling, including the credit counseling session required before a consumer can file for bankruptcy and the debtor education course required before discharge.
TCPA
Telephone Consumer Protection Act — the federal law governing when and how consumers can be called or texted, including permitted calling hours and consent requirements.
DNC
Do Not Call — both the National DNC Registry and NCCB's own internal do-not-call list. A request to stop calling must be honored on both.
DID
Direct Inward Dial — a dedicated phone number assigned to a rep or campaign. Deliverability rules like call-volume caps and carrier spam-flagging apply per DID.
Recycle (leads)
Returning a disconnected or bad number to the active dial list after a waiting period (30 days for this list), since debt clients on pay-as-you-go plans often reactivate.
A2P 10DLC
Application-to-Person 10-Digit Long Code — the registration framework carriers require before a business can send SMS marketing texts from a standard 10-digit number.
Metering (email)
Sending a large batch gradually over time instead of all at once, to protect sender/domain reputation.
TSR
FTC Telemarketing Sales Rule (16 CFR Part 310) — the federal rule requiring specific disclosures, prohibiting misrepresentations, governing the National DNC Registry, and restricting call abandonment.
EBR
Established Business Relationship — a limited exemption window during which a company may call a consumer despite National DNC listing, such as following an inquiry or application.
PEWC
Prior Express Written Consent — a documented, specific consumer authorization required for certain autodialed/prerecorded calls and texts.

Call Scripts

Reference scripts by service line — adapt the wording, keep the disclosures and consent language intact
Standing rule, every script, every call: only ask for a text number, email, or social handle after you've stated your name and NCCB Credit, and only after the consumer has said yes. Log the channel and the fact that they said yes as a note on the lead the same call — that note is your record that consent was given. Never add a number or handle to a marketing list because you found it, guessed it, or pulled it from a lead source. TrustedForm consent covers the original contact; it does not cover texting a number the consumer didn't provide for texting, or reaching out on a social platform they didn't name.

Opening

"Hi, is this [First Name]? This is [Rep Name] with NCCB Credit, calling about the debt help request you submitted a little while back. Do you have about five minutes right now, or is there a better time to talk?"

Discovery

"To see what we can put together for you, can you walk me through what you're carrying right now — roughly how many accounts, and about what total? And what's driving the request today — is it the interest, the number of payments, a specific creditor, or something else?"

Fee disclosure (say this before anything else about enrollment)

"Before we go further, here's exactly how this works cost-wise: we'll get your My800Scores credit monitoring started at $39.99 a month, and based on what you've told me, the Debt Management Program looks like the right fit — that's a $559 retainer plus a 10% success fee on the total debt enrolled. I want you to have those numbers up front, not find them later."

Always disclose the monitoring fee and the program fee together, in the same call, before describing enrollment steps. Which program applies (Credit Counseling, Credit Restoration, Debt Settlement, or Debt Management) depends on the answers from the Service Advisory Determination — see the Service Advisory Guide tab for the full decision tree and all four fee structures. Don't assume Debt Management is the answer before walking through it.

Requesting contact info / socials (compliant phrasing)

"If we get disconnected or I need to send you anything to review, is it okay if I text this same number? And if a text or call doesn't reach you, what's the best way to follow up — email, or do you check something like WhatsApp or Facebook Messenger more often?"

Wait for a clear yes before adding any channel. Log it: "Consumer confirmed OK to text [number] and reach via [channel] — verbal consent given [date/time]."

Handling urgency

"You mentioned [the specific pressure they named] — that's exactly the kind of thing this plan is built to get ahead of, so let's not let it sit. What would it take for you to feel ready to move forward today?"

If the consumer named a deadline, threat of suit, repossession, or similar urgency earlier in the call, reflect it back specifically here rather than moving straight to a generic close.

Close

"Here's what happens next: I'll get your agreement over to you today, we'll set up your first counseling session, and I'll go ahead and put a follow-up on the calendar for [date/time] so nothing falls through the cracks — does that work for you?"

Log the note and push the calendar appointment before you hang up, not after.

Opening

"Hi [First Name], this is [Rep Name] with NCCB Credit. You'd reached out about getting some help with your credit report — do you have a few minutes to go over what's on there?"

Discovery

"What's the credit goal driving this — are you working toward a mortgage, a car, or just trying to clean things up in general? And have you pulled your reports recently, or would that be a good first step?"

Required disclosures (CROA)

State these plainly, before any fee is discussed:

  • No fee is collected before the promised service is actually performed.
  • The consumer has the right to cancel, without penalty, until midnight of the third business day after signing.
  • No specific results or score increase can be promised or guaranteed.
  • The consumer already has the right to dispute inaccurate items on their own, for free, directly with the credit bureaus.

"Everything we do is in writing, you can cancel with no penalty in the first three business days, and I can't promise you a specific score or that anything specific comes off — what I can walk you through is the process and what's realistic."

Fee disclosure

"This falls under our Credit Restoration Program — it's $35 per account, no retainer fee, with a minimum initial escrow deposit of $105 covering the first 3 accounts."

See the Service Advisory Guide tab for the full fee schedule and how this program compares to Credit Counseling, Debt Settlement, and Debt Management.

Requesting contact info / socials (compliant phrasing)

"I'll be sending your review over in writing — is it alright if I email it to this address, and text you if I need a quick answer on something? If there's a better way to reach you day to day, let me know and I'll use that instead."

Log the confirmed channel(s) the same way as the debt management script.

Close

"I'll get the written agreement and the cancellation notice over to you today. Take your time reading it — once you're ready to sign, we'll get your file started. I'll put a check-in on the calendar for [date/time] either way."

Opening

"Hi [First Name], this is [Rep Name] with NCCB Credit. I understand you're looking into credit counseling — is this in connection with a bankruptcy filing, or more general budget and debt counseling?"

Discovery

"Have you already spoken with an attorney about filing, or are you still deciding? And has anyone mentioned the pre-filing counseling session or the post-filing debtor education course to you yet?"

If they're pursuing bankruptcy, be clear this call is educational and does not replace legal advice — do not answer questions about whether to file Chapter 7 vs. 13, exemptions, or timing; refer those to their attorney.

What to explain

"Before a bankruptcy case can be filed, federal law requires a credit counseling session from an approved agency, usually within the 180 days before filing. After the case is filed, there's a second requirement — a debtor education course — before the debt can be discharged. I can walk you through scheduling both."

Requesting contact info / socials (compliant phrasing)

"I'll need to send you a certificate after each session for your attorney or the court — is email still the best way to get that to you, and is it okay if I text a reminder before your scheduled session?"

Close

"Let's get your first session on the calendar — does [date/time] work? I'll send the confirmation and make sure you have everything you need for your attorney."

Standard Voicemail Script

"Hi [First Name], this is [Rep Name] with NCCB Credit. I'm following up on the request you submitted about [debt help / credit repair / credit counseling]. Give me a call back at [callback number] when you get a chance — I'll try you again in a few days if I don't hear back. Thanks, and I hope you have a good day."

Do

  • State your name and NCCB Credit within the first breath — don't make them guess who's calling.
  • Say what the call is about in general terms (their request, their file) without disclosing account or debt specifics — voicemail isn't a secure channel.
  • Leave one clear callback number, said once, at a normal pace.
  • Keep it under 20-25 seconds. Long voicemails get deleted halfway through.
  • Log that you left a voicemail as a note the same call, and set the status to Left Voicemail so the next touch in the cadence knows where things stand.

Don't

  • Don't mention a specific dollar amount, account number, or creditor name in the message.
  • Don't say anything that sounds like a threat, a deadline, or urgency pressure — that's the kind of message that gets forwarded to a friend or posted online.
  • Don't leave more than one voicemail in the same day, and don't leave one on every single attempt — a voicemail on the first and third tries of a cadence is plenty.
  • Don't repeat the callback number more than once — it reads as pushy, not helpful.
  • Don't use a script that sounds pre-recorded or robotic — say it like a person leaving a normal message, not reading a disclosure out loud.

Daily Training & Cadence

Call, text, and email cadence and tactics — review one section per huddle, then rotate back through as a refresher

1. Telemarketing Tactics

Cadence

  • Call each lead 3 times per day for 2 weeks.
  • Stagger call times so the same lead is reached at a different time of day on each attempt — a lead that never answers a 10am call may pick up at 4pm. Rotate your own call blocks (morning / midday / late afternoon) across the week rather than always dialing the same list at the same hour.
  • Recycle disconnected numbers after 30 days, not sooner — many debt clients are on pay-as-you-go cell plans, so a disconnected number today may be reactivated within a month. Tag disconnected numbers with a recycle date instead of closing the lead outright.

Deliverability

  • Keep each DID under 50 calls per day, per carrier. This is a spam-flagging threshold, not a soft target — carriers start marking a DID "Spam Likely" once it crosses this volume, which tanks answer rates for every rep sharing that number.
  • Daily huddle check-in: each rep confirms their assigned DID's dial count before end of shift so no single number gets overloaded.

Script

  • Use the aged-leads script for this list — these are people who already applied online, not cold leads. Open by referencing their prior application rather than a generic intro; it re-establishes context and shortens the path to a real conversation.
  • The aged-leads script changes the hook, not the compliance steps — reps still follow the correct My800Scores enrollment and Service Advisory Determination (see the Service Advisory Guide tab for current fees) once the conversation moves past the opener.

2. Texting Tactics

Cadence

  • Treat texting as an auxiliary to calling, not a replacement. Cap at 2 texts per week per lead.
  • Text as a follow-up nudge after an unanswered call, not as the first touch.

Deliverability

  • Every DID used for texting must be registered on a verified A2P 10DLC campaign. Unregistered or unverified numbers get filtered or blocked by carriers — this is a compliance and deliverability requirement, not optional paperwork.

Script

  • Request sample scripts before the campaign goes live and route them through the daily training rotation.

3. Emailing Tactics

Cadence

  • Once per week. Tuesdays, 10:00 a.m.–2:00 p.m. show the highest open rate — anchor the weekly send inside that window.

Deliverability

  • Domain management is the single biggest lever here. Avoid known spam-trigger words in subject lines and body copy.
  • Meter sends over time (warm-up pacing) rather than blasting the full list at once — this is what protects domain reputation long-term.

Script

  • Content quality is critical — on average 40% of consumers unsubscribe when content feels irrelevant to them. Segment messaging to the lead's actual situation where possible, rather than sending one generic template to the full 4,000-lead list.

Compliance References

Reference points only — not legal advice. Confirm anything client-facing with NCCB's compliance contact or outside counsel before relying on it.

TCPA — Telephone Consumer Protection Act

RequirementWhat it means on a call
Calling hoursCalls only between 8:00 AM and 9:00 PM in the consumer's local time zone — not the rep's.
National Do Not Call RegistryGenerally can't be called for marketing unless an exemption applies (see EBR below). Scrub call lists against the registry at least every 31 days.
Established Business Relationship (EBR)An inquiry or application — like these McGraw aged leads — can create a limited window to still call a DNC-listed number: commonly cited as roughly 3 months from inquiry, or up to 18 months from a completed transaction.
Internal/company DNCIf a lead ever says "don't call me again," that goes on NCCB's internal suppression list immediately and permanently, regardless of National Registry status or EBR timing — this overrides the 3-touches-per-day cadence for that lead.
Consent for texts/autodialed callsTexting a number, or calling it with an autodialer or prerecorded message, needs the consumer's prior express written consent (PEWC) for that specific channel — phone consent doesn't automatically cover texting, and texting runs on its own compliance track (A2P 10DLC registration, its own opt-out handling).
Call abandonmentIf using a dialer, keep abandoned-call rates within the TSR limit (industry standard: under 3%), and make sure any answered-but-abandoned call plays the required disclosure message.
RecordkeepingRetain call records, including consent and DNC-suppression records — treat 5 years as the working retention baseline pending compliance counsel confirmation.
Identify yourselfState your name, that you're calling on behalf of NCCB Credit, and a callback number early in the call.

Texas-Specific Calling Hours

Texas Business & Commerce Code § 301.051 layers its own restriction on top of the federal window above, including a later Sunday start (commonly cited as noon). Where state and federal rules differ, follow whichever is more restrictive for that call.

Texas Finance Code Chapter 394 (OCCC) — Debt Management Services

Governs NCCB's debt management licensing. Before a consumer can be enrolled in a debt management plan, a certified counselor must have:

  • Provided individualized counseling and educational information covering household finances, managing credit and debt, and budgeting.
  • Prepared an individualized financial analysis and an initial debt management plan with specific recommendations.
  • Determined the consumer has a reasonable ability to make the proposed payments.

A debt management services agreement can't be prepared or presented until these steps are complete — this is part of why the correct order (My800Scores enrollment, then Service Advisory Determination — see the Service Advisory Guide tab for current program fees) matters as a compliance sequence, not just internal policy.

The agreement itself must be in writing and include, among other items: the provider's name, address, and phone; participating creditors and payment schedule; permitted fees (Chapter 394 sets maximum amounts — never quote or imply fees outside NCCB's approved schedule); a statement that the plan may affect the consumer's credit favorably or unfavorably; and a statement that either party may cancel without penalty on 10 days' notice, with a refund of undisbursed funds.

If a call is conducted primarily in a language other than English, performance can't begin until a written agreement in that language is signed and provided to the consumer. NCCB's OCCC registration and surety bond/insurance must stay current — reps should know NCCB operates under this registration if a consumer asks "are you licensed?"

CROA — Credit Repair Organizations Act

  • No fee may be collected before the promised service is actually performed — no advance/upfront fees.
  • A written contract is required, describing the services, total cost, and timeframe.
  • The consumer must receive a written notice of the right to cancel, without penalty, until midnight of the third business day after signing.
  • No guarantee of a specific result (score increase, deletion of a specific item) may be made.
  • The consumer must be told, in writing, that they can dispute inaccurate credit report items directly and for free, and that free credit counseling alternatives exist.

Bankruptcy Counseling Requirements

Federal law requires an approved credit counseling session before a bankruptcy case can be filed (generally within the 180 days before filing), and a separate debtor education course after filing and before discharge. This call is educational only — questions about whether or when to file, or which chapter to file under, should be referred to the consumer's attorney.

These are reference summaries of publicly available regulatory material, not a compliance manual and not legal advice. Requirements change and vary by exact fact pattern — verify current language and applicability with NCCB's compliance contact or legal counsel before using any of this in a client-facing context.
